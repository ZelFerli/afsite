🧖‍♀️ The Spa & Glow Guide

Japanese self-care is all about "O-mote-nashi" (hospitality). Treat yourself — you deserve it!

🦶

Ashiyu Cafes (Foot Spas!)

In neighborhoods like Ueno, you can find cafes where you soak your feet in a hot spring tub while drinking matcha latte! The water is naturally heated and mineral-rich — perfect for tired walking feet.

💡 Tip: Bring a small towel and wear easy-off shoes or sandals!

#FootSpa #Relaxation
💆‍♀️

Gokuraku-yu (Super Sento)

It's like a water park for relaxation! Try the "Soda Bath" (it tingles like carbonation on your skin!), the "Sulfur Bath" for soft skin, or the "Jet Bed" where you float on water jets.

💡 Tip: Tattoos are sometimes not allowed — check ahead online.

#OnsenVibes #MustTry
🌿

The Head Spa Trend

Tokyo has exploded with "head spas" — a deeply relaxing scalp treatment that lasts 30–60 mins. Stylists massage your scalp, steam your hair, and use special oils. It's pure bliss!

Where to look: Search "ヘッドスパ" on Google Maps in Shinjuku or Shibuya.

🛁

Koiwai Farm Hotel Bath

Some ryokans (traditional inns) even near Tokyo have private baths called "kashikiri onsen" — you rent the entire bath for yourself or your group! Pure luxury.

💡 Tip: Book in advance — these fill up fast!

🛍️ J-Beauty Scavenger Hunt — Go to Matsumoto Kiyoshi or Sundrug stores!

  • Hada Labo Gokujyun Lotion: The "Holy Grail" of Japanese skincare. Mega-hydration in a bottle. Get the pink cap (light) or blue cap (extra)!
  • Canmake Marshmallow Finish Powder: Cult-favorite setting powder for a soft, airbrushed look. Only ~¥700!
  • DHC Lip Cream: The best drugstore lip balm in Japan. Stock up — friends will BEG you for one.
  • Biore UV Aqua Rich Sunscreen: The most beloved sunscreen in Asia. Watery texture, no white cast, perfect under makeup.
  • Peach Leaf Bath Salts: Make your hotel tub smell like a sakura garden. Perfect souvenir for yourself!
  • Lululun Face Masks: A big colorful box of daily face masks for ¥1,000. Try the "Hokkaido Rose" edition!
🌸 Japanese Skincare Philosophy: "Mochi Skin" The goal of Japanese beauty is "mochi hada" — skin as soft and bouncy as a rice cake. It's all about hydration layering: lotion → essence → serum → moisturizer. Less makeup, more glow!

🥐 Trendy Bakeries, Cafes & Sweet Treats

Tokyo bakeries are famous for being "too pretty to eat" — but you absolutely should eat them.

🥐

Truffle Mini (Shio-Pan Bakery)

The legendary "Salt Bread" (Shio-pan) — buttery, salty, and shaped like a crescent. People line up for HOURS. Go at opening time (usually 7am). They also do truffle cream puffs!

#WorthTheWait
🪵

Shiro-Hige's Cream Puff Factory

The only bakery authorized to make cream puffs shaped like Totoro! Hidden in a quiet, leafy neighborhood (Setagaya). Cream puff flavors rotate seasonally — spring means sakura!

#Totoro #HiddenGem
🍡

Harajuku Takeshita Street Treats

The OG of Tokyo sweet chaos! Try the Rainbow Cotton Candy, Crepes stuffed with matcha ice cream, and the wild Monster Milkshakes. Budget ~¥500–¥1,500 per treat.

  • Marion Crepes — the original since 1976!
  • Totti Candy Factory (rainbow cotton candy)
  • Egg 'n Things for fluffy pancakes
🍵

Matcha Everything!

Tokyo has elevated matcha to an art form. Don't leave without trying:

  • Nana's Green Tea — matcha tiramisu 🤤
  • Itohkyuemon — 100% pure matcha soft serve
  • % Arabica (Omotesando) — the most aesthetic coffee shop
  • Matcha latte in a vending machine (yes, really!)

📍 Nakameguro Starbucks Reserve Roastery

Widely called "the most beautiful Starbucks in the world." Four floors, a tea room with rare loose-leaf teas, cocktail bar, and a terrace that overlooks the canal lined with sakura trees. Not your average PSL situation. Plan to spend an hour here — there are cozy nooks on every floor!

💡 Pro Tip: The line to get in can be 30 mins on weekends. Visit on a weekday morning!

#Aesthetic #MustVisit #CanalViews
🍞 Bakery Culture Tip: The Morning Hunt Japanese bakeries ("pan-ya") often sell out by noon! The trick is to grab a tray and tongs at the door, load up like you're at a buffet, and pay at the end. It's a fun ritual — no menu needed, just point at what looks good!

🖋️ Stationery & Sticker Heaven

If you love pens, journals, washi tape, and stickers — you might need to buy an extra suitcase.

Itoya Ginza 🏢 12 Floors!

The ultimate stationery destination. One whole floor is just for paper — Japanese "washi" paper in hundreds of textures and colors. You can design your own custom notebook at their "Ginza Planner" bar. The basement has fresh flowers. The rooftop has a garden. It's incredible.

💡 Don't miss: The "Traveler's Note" section on floor 6 and the gorgeous Japanese ink sets on floor 4.

#StatNerd #GinzaGems

Tokyu Hands (Now called "Hands") 🎨

Think of it as a craft store that exploded in the best way. Go to the "DIY and Hobby" floors — they have stickers of every cat breed, food item, celebrity face, and Japanese sign imaginable. Also incredible for: washi tape, rubber stamps, calligraphy supplies, and weird gadgets you didn't know you needed.

💡 Secret weapon: The store map is on a card at the entrance — grab one or you'll be wandering for hours (not necessarily a bad thing).

#WashiTapeHeaven #MustBrowse

Traveler's Factory (Nakameguro) ✈️

A tiny magical shop inside an old paper factory building. It's THE home of the iconic "Traveler's Notebook" — a handstitched leather journal with removable inserts. It feels like a secret club for people who love to travel and write. The staff stamps your notebook with the date of your visit!

💡 Limited editions: They release Tokyo-exclusive refills you literally can't get anywhere else on Earth.

🖊️

Kinokuniya Bookstore

The flagship in Shinjuku has a whole floor dedicated to art books, journals, and premium stationery. Perfect for design-lovers. They also carry English books!

🎁

¥100 Daiso Finds

Don't sleep on Daiso (Japan's 100-yen store)! They stock adorable sticker sheets, mini notebooks, washi tape sets, and stamp pads for just ¥110 each. Best souvenir value in Tokyo!

✏️ What to Buy for Friends Back Home A curated stationery gift box from Tokyo is the ultimate thoughtful souvenir! Aim for: 1 pack washi tape, 1 set of gel pens (Muji or Pilot Juice), a few sticker sheets, and a small decorative clip or charm. Total cost: ~¥1,500. Priceless reaction guaranteed.

🐱 The Cat-Lover's Complete Guide

Tokyo takes its cat obsession seriously — from roaming street cats to museum exhibitions!

😻

Cat Cafe Mocha Most Popular

The most "Instagrammable" cat cafe in Tokyo. Birdcage-style hanging seats, massive wooden climbing trees, and over 20 cats of different breeds. Drinks included with your admission. Book online in advance — they fill up fast on weekends!

Price: ~¥1,500 for 30 mins (includes one drink)

#CatCafe
🏘️

Yanaka Ginza — "Cat Town"

An old Showa-era shopping street where real cats roam freely like they own the place (they do). Every shop sells cat-shaped snacks — try the cat-tail donuts ("nekoze donut")! Also home to adorable handmade cat goods from local artists.

Best time to visit: Late afternoon — that's when the cats come out to watch the sunset.

🎭

Gotokuji Temple (The Lucky Cat Temple!)

This is where the "Maneki-neko" (beckoning lucky cat) was born! The temple grounds are filled with hundreds of ceramic lucky cats donated by visitors. It's magical, photogenic, and deeply peaceful. Located in Setagaya.

💡 Tip: You can buy a small ceramic cat to leave as an offering!

🛍️

Cat Shopping Spots

Where to find the best cat merchandise:

  • Nekobukuro (in Ikebukuro Tokyu Hands) — rooftop cat area + cat gift shop
  • Shimokitazawa vintage shops — hand-painted cat tote bags
  • Harajuku Laforet — cat-themed fashion accessories
  • Any Don Quijote — cat plushies of every size and breed

💡 Cat Cafe Etiquette — The Golden Rules!

  • Do NOT pick up the cats — let them come to you. Patience is rewarded!
  • Swap your shoes for the provided slippers at the door — always!
  • Photos are okay, but absolutely NO flash photography.
  • Wash your hands before AND after — they usually have a sanitizing station.
  • Don't wake sleeping cats, no matter how cute they look.
  • Keep voices soft and low — the cats appreciate calm energy.
🐾 The Secret to Getting Cats to Come to You Sit very still on the floor or a low seat. Look away from the cat (direct eye contact feels threatening to them). Hold out one relaxed hand, palm down, at floor level. Within a few minutes, curiosity wins every time. Slow blink at them — that's how cats say "I love you"!

🌸 The Pink List: Sakura Season Guide

Sakura (cherry blossom) season is magical but SHORT — usually late March to mid-April. Plan around it!

🛶 Inokashira Park (Kichijoji)

Rent a swan boat and float under the blooms! The trees hang SO low over the water that petals literally fall into your lap. One of the most romantic spots in all of Tokyo. Arrive early — boat rental lines get long by 10am.

💡 Bonus: The Ghibli Museum is a 5-min walk away!

#SwanBoat #MostRomantic

🧺 Yoyogi Park

The best spot for people-watching under the blossoms. On weekends you'll see synchronized dancers, elaborate picnics, dogs in strollers, and groups of friends doing karaoke under the trees. Pure Tokyo joy. Bring a tarp and snacks!

💡 Tip: Blue tarps are reserved with GREAT seriousness — never step on someone's tarp!

#PicnicVibes

🌙 Chidorigafuchi (Moat Walk)

The famous nighttime sakura (yozakura) walk! The moat around the Imperial Palace is lit up at night, reflecting pink blossoms in the dark water below. It's absolutely breathtaking. Best visited after 7pm when the crowd thins slightly.

🏯 Shinjuku Gyoen

A large, manicured national garden with MULTIPLE varieties of cherry blossoms that bloom at slightly different times — meaning the season lasts longer here! Also one of the few parks where you can drink alcohol under the trees (BYO sake!).

🍦 Sakura Flavors: Everything to Try This Season

  • Sakura Soft Serve: Tastes like floral vanilla with a pink swirl. Find at konbini or specialty dessert shops.
  • Sakura Latte: Starbucks and Tully's release limited seasonal sakura lattes every spring — GET THEM!
  • Sakura Mochi: Pink rice cake wrapped in a pickled cherry leaf. The leaf is edible and gives a unique salty-sweet flavor.
  • Sakura Kit-Kats: The perfect souvenir! Also try: matcha, wasabi, sake, and sweet potato flavors.
  • Hanami Bento: Convenience stores release special spring bento boxes around sakura season — colorful, delicious, ~¥600!
  • Sakura Cream Bread: Lawson's seasonal cherry blossom-filled bread bun is legendary. Check daily — sells out fast.
📱 Track the Blooms! Use the app "Sakura Navi" or check the Japan Meteorological Corporation's "cherry blossom forecast" online. The bloom moves north from Kyushu to Hokkaido — Tokyo usually peaks in late March or early April. Being there at full bloom (mankai) vs. 50% bloom is a totally different experience!

🍣 Sushi, Ramen & Street Food Bible

Tokyo has more Michelin-starred restaurants than any other city on Earth — but some of the best food costs under ¥1,000!

🐟

Tsukiji Outer Market

Don't let anyone tell you the inner market closing ruined Tsukiji — the outer market is still incredible! Get there by 8am for the freshest experience. Must-eats:

  • Grilled wagyu beef on a stick (~¥500)
  • Strawberry mochi daifuku (~¥200 each)
  • Rolled omelet Tamagoyaki — try it fresh from the pan!
  • Uni (sea urchin) on a spoon — if you're adventurous!
🍣

Hanamaru Sushi (Ginza / Shinjuku)

"Standing sushi" (kaiten-zushi) from Hokkaido. Ultra fresh and surprisingly affordable. Each plate is ¥110–¥330 on the belt!

Order these specifically:

  • Seared salmon with cheese (Japan-only!)
  • Ootoro (fatty tuna) — splurge on it here
  • Ikura (salmon roe) gunkan — it pops!
  • Tamago (egg) sushi — sweet and soft
🍜

Ramen 101 — Pick Your Style

Each region has its own ramen! In Tokyo:

  • Shoyu (soy sauce): Classic Tokyo style, clear broth, punchy
  • Tonkotsu: Rich creamy pork bone broth (Fuunji, Shinjuku)
  • Tsukemen: Dipping noodles! Noodles on the side, dip into thick broth
  • Tantanmen: Spicy sesame noodle soup

💡 Tip: Most ramen shops use a ticket machine at the door. Point at pictures!

🥟

Street Food & Konbini Finds

Never underestimate 7-Eleven! Japan's konbini are a whole experience:

  • Onigiri (rice balls) — get the tuna mayo flavor first
  • Egg salad sandwiches — life-changing
  • Oden in winter (hot pot from a warm tank!)
  • Chicken karaage from the hot food counter
  • The ice cream section — every visit is a new discovery
🥢 Chopstick & Dining Etiquette — Know Before You Go!
  • Never pass food from chopsticks to chopsticks — it mimics a funeral ritual!
  • Don't stick chopsticks upright in rice — also a funeral symbol.
  • Slurping noodles is a compliment — go for it!
  • Say "Itadakimasu" before eating — it means "I humbly receive this meal."
  • Tipping is NOT done in Japan — it can actually be seen as rude.
  • When the server brings the bill, you pay at the register, not at the table.

🗺️ Neighborhood Personality Guide

Tokyo's neighborhoods each have a completely distinct vibe — here's the cheat sheet!

🎀 Harajuku

Vibe: Wild fashion, youth culture, iconic street food

  • Takeshita Street for crepes & cotton candy
  • Ura-Harajuku for vintage & indie designers
  • Omotesando for luxury shopping & % Arabica coffee
  • Cat Street for cool boutiques and street art
#Fashion #Kawaii

🌃 Shinjuku

Vibe: Neon lights, massive department stores, nightlife

  • Kabukicho for the famous neon Godzilla head!
  • Golden Gai — tiny bars with 5 seats each
  • Omoide Yokocho (Memory Lane) for yakitori under the trains
  • Free observation deck at Tokyo Metropolitan Gov. Building
#Nightlife #Foodie

🌿 Shimokitazawa

Vibe: Bohemian, indie music, vintage fashion, coffee

  • Best vintage clothing shops in all of Tokyo
  • Tiny live music venues down every alley
  • Incredible brunch cafe culture
  • "Bonus Track" — an outdoor market street with indie shops
#Vintage #IndieCool

🏮 Asakusa

Vibe: Traditional, historic, Senso-ji Temple, craft shopping

  • Senso-ji Temple — go at dawn before crowds arrive
  • Nakamise shopping street for traditional sweets & souvenirs
  • Rent a kimono and walk the old streets!
  • Rickshaw rides available for ~¥3,000
#Traditional #Historic

🎮 Akihabara

Vibe: Anime, manga, electronics, gaming

  • Multi-floor arcades — try the UFO catcher machines!
  • Animate for anime merch & manga
  • Maid cafes (staff in maid outfits, ~¥1,000 cover)
  • Retro game shops — find old Gameboy cartridges!
#Anime #Gamer

☕ Nakameguro

Vibe: Sophisticated, canal walks, designer boutiques, coffee

  • Canal walk lined with boutiques and cafes
  • Starbucks Reserve Roastery
  • Traveler's Factory stationery shop
  • Best neighborhood for sakura season walks
#Aesthetic #Peaceful
🚇 Getting Around Like a Local Get a Suica or Pasmo IC card at any airport or train station — tap in and out of trains, buses, AND pay at konbinis with it! The Tokyo Metro is the cleanest, most punctual transit system in the world. Trains arrive literally to the second. Download "Google Maps" or "Japan Official Travel App" for navigation.

📱 Quick Reference & Survival Phrases

🗣️ Essential Japanese Phrases

  • Sumimasen — Excuse me / Sorry
  • Arigatou gozaimasu — Thank you very much
  • Ikura desu ka? — How much is this?
  • Kore wo kudasai — I'll take this one, please
  • Doko desu ka? — Where is ___?
  • Toire wa doko? — Where's the bathroom?
  • Itadakimasu! — Said before eating ("I receive")
  • Oishii! — Delicious!
  • Kawaii! — Cute!
  • Eigo wa hanasemasu ka? — Do you speak English?

⚠️ Things NOT to Do in Tokyo

  • Don't eat while walking (bad manners!)
  • Don't talk on your phone on the train
  • Don't tip at restaurants or cafes
  • Don't jaywalk — people really don't!
  • Don't litter (there are barely any trash cans — carry a bag!)
  • Don't point at people with your finger
